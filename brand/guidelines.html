01 · The brand

A hospitality brand, powered by quiet technology

Tawilati connects guests, owners, kitchen staff and waiters around one table. A guest scans the QR code, orders from their phone and follows the order. The restaurant team runs menus, tables, the kitchen queue and the bill from the same system. The brand should feel like a well-run café: warm, calm and confident, with the technology in the background.

ElegantSimple forms, generous space, nothing extra.
WarmCream, forest green and amber. Café materials, not chrome.
TrustworthyClear prices, clear status, a bill that adds up.
ConnectedGuest, kitchen and waiter see the same order.
ApproachableAnyone can use it without instructions.

Tawilati looks like

  • A carefully designed hospitality brand
  • Modern software that stays out of the way
  • Timeless rather than trendy

Tawilati never looks like

  • A generic AI startup or futuristic tech platform
  • A traditional restaurant website
  • A template with gradients, glow or glassmorphism

02 · Logo

The cup and the signal

The mark puts a Wi-Fi signal over a coffee cup. The cup stands for hospitality and the dining experience. The amber signal stands for the connection between guests and the restaurant. Together they say: good service, made smoother by technology.

+ =

Lockups

Use the horizontal logo by default. Use the stacked logo where space is tall or centred, such as table cards and print. Use the icon alone only where the name already appears nearby or space is very small.

Horizontal · landing pages, desktop navigation, documents

Stacked · QR table cards, print, promotional assets

Reverse · cream lettering and cup, amber signal, on Forest Green only

Icon · compact navigation, app icon, favicon

Clear space

Keep a margin at least the height of the cup (x) on every side. Nothing else enters that space: no text, edges or other logos.

xx

Minimum sizes

Below these sizes the signal arcs merge. Under 24 px use favicon.svg, which has two arcs and heavier lines.

Misuse

Always use the supplied SVG files. Never redraw, edit or decorate the logo.

Don't rotate it

Don't recolour it outside the palette

Don't add shadows, gradients or 3D

Don't place it on low-contrast colours

Balance

Most of any screen is cream or white. Green and charcoal carry the structure, sage softens it, and amber appears only in small doses.

60% cream and white · 25% forest green and charcoal · 10% sage · 5% amber

Text colours and contrast

Text must reach 4.5 : 1 against its background (WCAG AA). Amber and sage are too light for text on cream, so each has a darker text version. Use amber and sage as fills, borders and decoration; use the ink versions when the colour has to be a word.

05 · Iconography

Clean line icons

Icons follow Lucide: a 24 px grid, 2 px strokes, rounded ends and corners. Four icons that Lucide lacks (menu, tables, kitchen, reports) are drawn to the same rules. Colour icons Forest Green or Charcoal.

06 · UI components

Calm, clear controls

Components use white cards on cream, light borders, soft 10 to 16 px corners and almost no shadow. Forest Green marks the one action that matters most on each screen.

Buttons

Primary for the main action (Submit order, Close tab). Secondary for supporting actions. Accent only for promotions and highlights, never as the main call to action. Ghost for cancel and back. Buttons are at least 48 px tall for touch.

Text fields

Enter a rate above 0, for example 89,500.

08 · Imagery

Real tables, real food

Photography should feel like a good afternoon in a café: natural light, warm wood, real plates and a little greenery. Food photos on the menu are square, shot from slightly above, on a calm background.

Use

  • Authentic restaurant interiors in natural light
  • Warm wooden textures and elegant table settings
  • High-quality, honest food photography
  • Subtle greenery and neutral materials

Avoid

  • Robots, holograms and futuristic imagery
  • Generic AI graphics or illustrations
  • Oversaturated stock photos
  • Busy backgrounds that compete with the food

09 · Voice

Clear, friendly, professional, confident, helpful and simple. Write what the person needs to know or do next. Skip technical terms and error codes.
